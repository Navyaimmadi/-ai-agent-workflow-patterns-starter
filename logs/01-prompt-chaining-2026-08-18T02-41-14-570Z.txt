=== Step 2 - Final Lesson ===
## Mini‑Lesson: “What Is an AI Agent Workflow?”  
**Duration:** ~35 minutes (can be shortened or extended)

---

### 1. Objectives  
By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

| # | Learning Goal | Why It Helps |
|---|---------------|--------------|
| 1 | Define **AI agent** and **workflow** | Sets a shared vocabulary. |
| 2 | List the **main stages** an AI agent goes through | Gives a mental map of the process. |
| 3 | Explain how those stages work together to finish a task | Shows the big‑picture logic. |

---

### 2. What You’ll Need  
- A whiteboard, flip‑chart, or shared digital canvas (Google Jamboard, Miro®, etc.)  
- Sticky notes or colored markers (if in person)  
- A hand‑out with a short “scenario card” (e.g., *“Order a pizza”*) – optional but helpful.  

---

### 3. Quick Warm‑up (3 min)

| Prompt | What to Do | Goal |
|--------|------------|------|
| **Poll**: “What’s an AI to you?” | Ask a few volunteers to give a quick definition or example. | Activate prior knowledge and spark curiosity. |

---

### 4. 4‑Stage Definition (5 min)

> **AI agent**: *A computer system that can perceive its environment, make decisions, take actions, and learn from what happens.*

| Stage | What Happens | Everyday Analogy |
|-------|--------------|------------------|
| **Perception** | Collects data (inputs). | Reading an email |
| **Reasoning** | Processes data, plans, decides. | Choosing which pizza toppings fit your dietary needs |
| **Action** | Sends commands to the world. | Ordering the pizza over the phone |
| **Learning** | Uses feedback to improve. | Noting that the pizza burned, you’ll call at a different time next time |

> **Note:** Most frameworks add a *Feedback* or *Update* step that closes the loop.

---

### 5. Visual Workflow (6 min)

1. **Input**  
2. **Perception**  
3. **Reasoning**  
4. **Action**  
5. **Feedback** (optional but standard)  
6. **Update / Learning**

```
[Input] → [Perception] → [Reasoning] → [Action] → [Feedback] → [Update]
        ^-----------------------------------------------------------------|
```

- Use the board to draw a circular arrow showing the cycle.  
- Colour‑code each stage (e.g., blue = perception, green = reasoning).

---

### 6. One Mini‑Example: Ordering a Pizza (10 min)

> **Scenario**: You want to order a pizza for dinner, but you’re also planning a surprise birthday next week. You want the pizza to fit your current cravings, not the surprise plan.  

| Stage | How the AI Agent Works | What You Would See |
|-------|------------------------|--------------------|
| **Input** | The user (you) sends a request: “Order a pizza now.” | Text or voice command received by the AI. |
| **Perception** | The agent reads your past orders, dietary cues, and even the calendar to see you’re not busy next week. | Recognises “no conflict” in the schedule. |
| **Reasoning** | Decides on the best pizza (size, toppings) based on preferences and current offers. | Chooses a medium pepperoni pizza with a 10% discount. |
| **Action** | Places the order via a pizza‑delivery API and confirms. | “Pizza is on its way—expected in 35 minutes.” |
| **Feedback** | You receive the order confirmation and the delivery status. | “Delivered!” or “Delayed.” |
| **Update / Learning** | If the pizza arrives late, the model flags the delivery time for future predictions. | Adjusts its recommendation window. |

> **Takeaway**: Even a simple task involves a clear cycle of perception → reasoning → action → feedback → learning.  
> This cycle makes the agent smarter over time.

---

### 7. Quick Interactive Activity (6 min)

1. Pair up (or work in small groups).  
2. Pick a daily routine you’re familiar with (e.g., “Sync your phone with the cloud,” “Set an alarm”).  
3. On a sticky note or digital card, outline the **six stages** for that routine, using the pizza example as a model.  
4. Share one interesting finding (e.g., a non‑obvious stage, a feedback loop that could improve the task).

---

### 8. Reflection & Check‑off (2 min)

| Task | Instruction |
|------|-------------|
| **Quiz** | Match the stage to its description: *Perception, Reasoning, Action, Feedback, Update, Input.* (1 point each). |
| **Micro‑reflection** | Write a quick sentence: “Knowing the workflow will help me choose better tools for ___.” |
> Collect responses and note any common insights.  

---

### 9. Resources & Next Steps (2 min)

- **Books**: *AI for Everyone* (Andrew Ng) – Chapter 3 “AI workflows.”  
- **Videos**: “What Is an AI Agent?” – OpenAI Learning Hub, 5 min.  
- **Hands‑On**: Try a basic chatbot in Scratch (select Text → Control → Pen) and experiment with a simple input–output loop.  

---

### 10. Closing (1 min)

- Quick recap: *Input → Perception → Reasoning → Action → Feedback → Update*  
- Ask: “Name one task you’ll try to model as an AI agent next week.”  

---

**Total Time**: 35 minutes  

Feel free to stretch or compress any section based on the size of your group or the format of your class. Happy teaching!
